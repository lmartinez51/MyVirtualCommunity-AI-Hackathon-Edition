## PART I — Approved Creative Direction

### 1. Core Concept

**Remanencia** is the original name of the game's liminal, dimensional universe.

The dimension does not necessarily predate the real-world locations it contains. Instead, it gradually forms from the memories, histories, stories, experiences, and accumulated traces associated with places. A building, a street, an abandoned project, a demolished structure, or a place remembered by generations of people can leave a presence that becomes part of Remanencia.

These presences are not simple replicas. They can become independent, distorted, expanded, interconnected, or transformed versions of their real-world counterparts. The dimension's origin and complete nature remain mysterious.

#### Core Narrative Premise
- Remanencia is a parallel spatial reality formed from the accumulated memory of places.
- Its geography is not bound by physical distance, original architectural dimensions, historical timelines, or conventional building plans.
- A doorway may connect two geographically unrelated locations; a building may contain rooms that never existed; a demolished structure may continue to exist in Remanencia, preserving fragments of its former identity.
- The player discovers this reality through exploration, environmental storytelling, artifacts, and progressively connected local histories.

### 3. Design Pillars

#### A. Memory Becomes Geography
Places and their accumulated histories shape the physical structure of Remanencia. The dimension may contain architectural remnants, reconstructed historical spaces, unfinished structures, memories of former activities, distorted versions of existing buildings, and entirely fictional spaces generated from the combined histories of multiple places.

#### B. Recognizable Origins, Impossible Architecture
The player should occasionally recognize the architectural identity of a real location while discovering that its dimensional counterpart obeys different spatial rules. The objective is not photorealistic duplication, but recognizable historical and architectural inspiration transformed into an original explorable environment.

#### C. Exploration and Discovery
Environmental storytelling is as important as direct encounters. Players should be able to reconstruct the history of an area by examining objects, fictional documents, architectural transitions, environmental changes, and traces left by former occupants.

#### D. An Evolving Ecosystem
Remanencia is not populated solely by hostile monsters. Humans, insects, animals, plants, and fungi may all be affected by the dimensional anomalies associated with specific locations, allowing each territory to develop distinct ecosystems and biological characteristics.

#### E. No Mandatory Protagonist Identity
The player remains a first-person explorer without an imposed biography or fixed protagonist backstory. Players may explore, investigate, or pass over mysteries without being forced into a single narrative interpretation.

## PART II — Nivel 0: Hotel de las Palomas

### 4. Real-World Inspiration & Factual Boundary

The first major explorable area of Remanencia is inspired by the unfinished **Hotel de las Palomas**, associated with **Avenida Plutarco Elías Calles** in **Ciudad Juárez, Chihuahua** — a real-world architectural reference marked by incomplete construction, uncertain public narratives regarding its original purpose, and local stories surrounding its abandonment and development.

> **Mandatory Factual Boundary:**  
> - The real-world Hotel de las Palomas must **never** be represented as having been contaminated with radioactive material.  
> - Alleged radioactive construction materials and Cobalt-60 contamination are **not** established facts about the real building.  
> - Present-day ownership, construction status, redevelopment, occupancy, and physical accessibility must **not** be assumed from historical photographs or older descriptions.  
> - The game presents a **fictional dimensional counterpart** inside Remanencia; its fictional contamination narrative exists strictly within the game's fiction.

---

### 5. Dimensional Identity & Candidate Environmental Areas

**Nivel 0 — Hotel de las Palomas** is designed as the first major explorable territory inside Remanencia — not a tiny isolated hotel interior.

- **Conceptual Exploration Scope:** Encompasses the dimensional hotel as its primary architectural anchor, with potential expansion into the surrounding East block of the virtual community and adjoining service structures.
- **Unresolved Spatial Scale (`NEEDS REFERENCE`):** Exact geographic boundaries, floor counts, and architectural dimensions must be investigated and confirmed with creator-approved references before geometry authoring. **Do not invent measurements.**

**Candidate Environmental Zones *(design candidates — not confirmed features of the real building)*:**
1. Main entrance and reception
2. Lobby and common areas
3. Unfinished guest floors
4. Finished or partially furnished rooms
5. Service corridors
6. Stairwells and elevator shafts
7. Maintenance and storage areas
8. Parking structure
9. Service courtyard
10. Rooftop and upper construction areas
11. Basement or subterranean infrastructure
12. Dimensional corridors connecting otherwise unrelated structures

---

### 6. Spatial Distortion Rules

The dimensional hotel may violate real-world architectural constraints while remaining readable and navigable:
- A staircase may reach a floor that never existed in the real structure.
- Corridors may extend beyond the exterior footprint.
- Guest rooms may repeat while holding different objects and histories.
- Elevators may open onto impossible destinations.
- Windows may look out onto spaces that cannot physically exist outside.
- Service corridors may connect to unrelated buildings in the virtual community.
- Adjacent wings may appear to belong to different construction eras.

**Coherence Constraint:** Spatial distortion must remain coherent enough for players to build mental maps and recognize meaningful anomalies. **Do not turn Nivel 0 into random procedural geometry.**

---

### 7. Fictional Cobalt-60 Anomaly

#### Narrative Premise
Within the fictional lore of Nivel 0, a persistent local rumor alleges that the hotel's construction materials were contaminated with radioactive material associated with Cobalt-60. While exploring, players may encounter contradictory evidence, historical allusions, fictional notices, and anomalous biological remains. The true nature and origin of the anomaly do not need to be immediately explained.

#### Mandatory Scientific Distinction
- Real **Cobalt-60** is a radioactive isotope; it does **not** cause instantaneous mutations or transform living organisms into monsters.
- The biological transformations in Remanencia are **supernatural / fictional dimensional phenomena**.
- Cobalt-60 is strictly the **in-universe narrative association** attributed to the anomaly — never a scientifically accurate explanation of its fictional effects, and never presented as real radiation science.

---

### 8. Biological Transformation & Ecology *(Creative Directions — Not Implementation Specs)*

The dimensional anomaly affects multiple forms of life across Nivel 0:

- **Humans:** Progressive physical alterations; skin, tissue, and anatomical changes; cognitive or behavioral shifts; impaired recognition of familiar spaces; advanced irreversible transformations; and individuals retaining fragments of memory or identity. *Not every exposed human must become hostile or completely lose their identity.*
- **Insects:** Abnormal growth, altered exoskeletons, unusual colony structures, adaptation to anomalous zones, and novel relationships with fungal and plant life.
- **Animals:** Physical deformation, behavioral adaptation, unusual sensory responses, territorial behavior, and changes linked to prolonged exposure.
- **Plants:** Abnormal root systems, unusual growth patterns, adaptation to enclosed interiors, invasive penetration of concrete/masonry, and unusual interactions with contaminated surfaces and standing water.
- **Fungi:** Expansive mycelial networks colonizing structural surfaces, symbiotic interactions with other organisms, growth throughout abandoned rooms and infrastructure, and possible links to biological transformation.

---

### 9. Exposure as a Gameplay System *(Design Framework — Not Yet Implemented)*

Prolonged exposure to the dimensional anomaly may eventually lead to progressive biological transformation. Before specifying numbers, timers, or health bars, future system design should establish rules distinguishing six factors:

1. **Exposure intensity**
2. **Duration**
3. **Environmental conditions**
4. **Type of organism**
5. **Transformation stage**
6. **Reversibility or permanence**

**Design Guardrails:** Do **not** implement arbitrary instant transformations, timers, or health systems yet. Players must be able to read environmental warning signs and understand that lingering in anomalous zones carries progressive consequences.

## PART III — Art Direction & Environmental Storytelling

### 10. Distinctive Visual Identity

Remanencia must establish an original visual identity fully independent of the recognizable Backrooms / Kane Pixels aesthetic.

**Explicitly Avoid Reproducing This Combination:**
- Iconic mustard-yellow striped wallpaper
- Damp mustard-yellow carpet
- Conventional acoustic drop-ceiling grids
- The familiar yellow fluorescent office maze
- The recognizable ceiling no-clip drop associated with existing Backrooms media

**Approved Visual Direction (Unfinished Northern Mexican Urban Construction):**
- Exposed poured concrete, unfinished structural columns, and protruding reinforcing steel (rebar)
- Old ceramic tiles, weathered plaster, and damaged/peeling paint
- Industrial work lighting, construction barriers, and raw service infrastructure
- Water intrusion/staining, biological overgrowth (roots, moss, mycelial networks), and anomalous dimensional materials
- Recognizable architectural remnants transitioning into progressively impossible spaces, letting dread and intrigue emerge from the place itself rather than unrelated monster tropes

---

### 11. Environmental Storytelling & Fictional Document Rules

Environmental clues should convey that the hotel had an original purpose and an interrupted construction history:
- Construction blueprints/notes, incomplete architectural plans, and maintenance records
- Old room-numbering sequences, unfinished electrical runs, and furniture from mixed eras
- Personal belongings, evidence of prior occupants, fictional contamination notices, and biological anomalies

> **Fictional Document Boundary:** Do **not** design fictional notices to impersonate authentic government radiation warnings, official regulatory seals, or verified historical evidence. Clearly distinguish fictional in-game documents from real historical sources wherever confusion could arise.

## PART IV — Relationship to the Existing Game

### 12. Incremental Preservation of Existing Unity Work

The existing `unity-client/Assets/Scenes/BackroomsLevel1Scene.unity` contains working exploration geometry, 15 original/preserved artifacts, puzzle mechanics (Evidence Board PDN-00, Level-Ω Safe `654321`, *El Ojo del Umbral*, *El Umbral de Lasser*), and local progression hooks.

- Transform and repurpose this work **incrementally** rather than discarding it or rebuilding the scene solely to change its name.
- Audit and classify every existing element (`KEEP`, `ADAPT`, `REPLACE`, `REMOVE`, `UNKNOWN / NEEDS REFERENCE`) before executing scene modifications.

### 13. Mandatory Progression & Save Compatibility

Future Remanencia increments must protect:
- Existing local JSON saves (`Application.persistentDataPath`)
- Permanent discoveries, Discovery Journal entries, learned access codes, solved puzzles, unlocked milestones, and logical checkpoints
- Existing persisted progression identifiers (which may remain stable internally while player-facing labels are updated, or migrate via deterministic backward-compatible aliases in `ProgressionSaveData.Normalize()`)

### 14. Entry & Exit Transitions (`PENDING DESIGN REVIEW`)

Existing transitions between the real-world corridor, storm-drain network, and `BackroomsLevel1Scene.unity` (Building A South *Velo del Umbral* and Manhole #1 ceiling drop) require creator review before modification. Do not assume every existing transition will remain unchanged, and do not modify transition controllers or serialized fields until separately authorized.

## PART V — Reality, Historical & Cultural Integrity

### 15. Historical Integrity

Maintain an explicit distinction across five categories:
1. **Documented historical events**
2. **Local oral histories and attributed testimonies**
3. **Unverified urban legends**
4. **Fictional events created for the game**
5. **Dimensional anomalies and supernatural phenomena**

- The real-world Cobalt-60 incident in Ciudad Juárez may inform thematic context, but the game's fictional hotel contamination must **never** be portrayed as a documented consequence of that accident.
- Never associate real living persons, actual property owners, or real businesses with invented criminal, medical, or contamination allegations.

### 16. Cultural Integrity

Historical Indigenous languages, cosmologies, and names may inform future research, subject to strict ethical guardrails:
- Do **not** invent Indigenous translations or assign undocumented meanings to historical words.
- Do **not** flatten distinct Indigenous peoples into a single undifferentiated culture.
- Clearly distinguish documented historical terminology from scholarly hypotheses.
- Treat living Indigenous communities and sacred cultural concepts with respect.
- No Indigenous term or cultural concept is approved as the name of Remanencia at this stage.
